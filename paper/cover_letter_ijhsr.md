Dear Editors of the International Journal of High School Research,

I am submitting my manuscript, "Overclocking the p-bit: Stochastic Binary
Neural Network Inference with Autocorrelated sMTJ Randomness," for
consideration in IJHSR.

Stochastic magnetic tunnel junctions generate physically random bits at
roughly a hundred-thousandth of the energy of software random number
generators, which makes them a leading candidate for energy-efficient
probabilistic AI hardware. A long-standing design rule says these devices
must never be sampled faster than twice their natural fluctuation time.
My paper measures, for the first time, what that rule actually costs a
neural-network application -- and shows the answer depends on operating
mode: batch-style inference tolerates sampling 200x faster than the rule
with under one point of accuracy loss, while continuous streaming
inference collapses to chance, for a reason I identify and largely repair
with a training method. The result converts a fixed hardware constraint
into a quantitative design trade worth 2-8x throughput on existing
devices.

The study is fully computational (an exact telegraph-process simulation,
~2,600 logged runs, replicated across five independently trained networks
and two datasets), was conducted independently, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. The manuscript includes an AI-use disclosure
describing the role of AI tools in the work. The complete code and
per-run records will be made public upon publication.
